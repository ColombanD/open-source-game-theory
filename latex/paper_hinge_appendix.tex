\subsubsection{Hinge results}
\label{apx-subsubsection:Experiments-BaseBots-Hinge}
On the hinge side the counts are 24, 18 and 22, with 35 row actions $C$: the open question moves six cells, four of them to mutual cooperation (\texttt{DupocBot} stops exploiting the else-branch cooperators \texttt{DBot} and \texttt{EBot}) and two to mutual defection (\texttt{CupodBot}'s detector fires on \texttt{OBot}).

\paragraph{Robustness to the hinge.} Every number of \cref{section:Results-Base} takes the three hinge pairs at the floor side. We re-ran the same stages with them at the hinge side; \cref{tab:hinge-body} puts the two branches side by side. The qualitative answer does not move: there is still no pure ESS, the three Nash components are still present (joined by two mixed ones, both carried by \texttt{EBot}, which the hinge side turns into a reciprocator of \texttt{DupocBot}), the dominant replicator support is the same, and at strong selection \texttt{DupocBot} is still the only stochastically stable strategy, at every $M$. What moves is the margin. \texttt{DupocBot} no longer exploits \texttt{DBot} and \texttt{EBot}, so its row loses two units of the temptation payoff; \texttt{TitForTatBot}, which already cooperated with both, closes part of the gap and ties it at two of the nine points. The stationary share at $(100, 1)$ falls from 86\% to 71\%, the cooperative basin from 88\% to 81\%, a small all-defect basin \{\texttt{DefectBot}, \texttt{OBot}\} appears with 4.5\% of the starts, and the self-cooperating share of the long run drops from 0.92 to 0.84. One structural change is worth a sentence: on the hinge side \texttt{CupodBot}'s row of actions coincides with \texttt{DupocBot}'s (both cooperate with every reciprocator and defect on \texttt{DefectBot} and \texttt{OBot}), although their columns do not, since \texttt{DupocBot} exploits \texttt{CupodBot}; a behavior-reading signal cannot tell them apart there. The direction is the general one: on the hinge side the Löbian cooperator is \emph{more} cooperative and \emph{less} fit, because the cells it loses are the ones in which it exploited partners it could not read. The strong-selection claim of \cref{section:Results-Base} is therefore unconditional, and the share and the robustness count are the floor-side numbers of a bracket whose other end is 71\% and five of nine points.

\subsubsection{Channel comparison: behavioral versus syntactic blur}
\label{apx-subsubsection:Experiments-Transparency-Channels}
So far the signal was behavioral: a noisy record of how the opponent plays. What if the leak is a corrupted copy of its code instead? By construction, the syntactic family carries exactly as much information as the behavioral one at the same $t$; it only differs in \emph{which} programs it mixes up. \Cref{fig:tau-families-body} shows that on this zoo the difference is real but confined to the collapse band. The two families disagree on the stochastically stable set at 5 of the 36 grid points with $\alpha > 0$, all at $t \le 0.6$, and the realized cooperation differs by more than 0.05 at six points; the transparency at which cooperation collapses, and with it the transparency at which the cooperative equilibrium disappears, is the same under both channels at every $\alpha$ but one. The exception is $\alpha = 0.6$ at $t = 0.2$, where source blur keeps both the cooperative equilibrium and a cooperating population (0.94) and behavior blur has lost both (0.01). Wherever the two differ by more than 0.05 above the collapse, source blur is the \emph{more} forgiving channel: at $t = 0.2$ and $\alpha \in \{0.4, 0.5\}$ the population cooperates at 0.92 under source blur against 0.84 and 0.85 under behavior blur, and at $\alpha = 0.2$ it cooperates at 0.84 and 0.94 against 0.78 and 0.86 for $t \in \{0.4, 0.6\}$. The pair the syntactic family confuses that the behavioral one does not, \texttt{DupocBot} and \texttt{CupodBot}, is on this zoo too (one swapped action leaf, opposite dispositions), so the mechanism by which source blur could be the harsher channel is present; on an eight-bot roster its weight is not enough to make it so anywhere on the grid. We read this as a property of the roster, not of the channel: a cautious population on a larger zoo may well need more transparency under source blur than under behavior blur, but we do not observe it here.

\subsubsection{Aggregators under blur}
\label{apx-subsubsection:Experiments-Transparency-Aggregators}
How should a Löbian program \emph{vote} once identities blur? The zoo \texttt{body+natives} adds to \texttt{body} two programs that ask \texttt{DupocBot}'s question of every hypothesis, \enquote{does this hypothesis provably cooperate with me?}, and change only the vote: \texttt{MaxConfidenceBot} cooperates iff \emph{at least one} hypothesis of weight at least $\alpha$ passes the test, \texttt{MinConfidenceBot} iff \emph{every} hypothesis of weight at least $1-\alpha$ passes it, so that a single credible hypothesis that cannot be shown to cooperate vetoes. Both are behavioral and syntactic twins of \texttt{DupocBot}, so the comparison runs on the $\varepsilon$-uniform family, which has no twin ceiling. On \texttt{body+natives} under the $\varepsilon$-uniform family (\cref{table:tau-aggregators}), the Löbian class \{\texttt{DupocBot}, \texttt{Max}, \texttt{Min}\} holds nearly all of the long run in an even split as long as the signal is clear: the aggregator is irrelevant until blur reaches the thresholds. Below that the three separate in a fixed order. \texttt{MinConfidenceBot}, which any credible hypothesis it cannot certify vetoes, exits first; then, in a mid-blur band, \texttt{MaxConfidenceBot} alone holds the population, because the sum player is fooled into cooperating with blurred exploiters while the max player, which needs one hypothesis credible on its own, is not. The band is a band of \emph{survival}, not of cooperation: at the most cautious settings the sum and max players share the long run again, but as defectors, and the population's cooperation rate is near zero. Once identities blur, ambiguity aversion is what keeps a Löbian cooperator alive.

\subsubsection{Hinge results under partial transparency}
\label{apx-subsubsection:Experiments-Transparency-Hinge}
We re-ran the two body sweeps of \cref{section:Results-Transparency} with the three hinge pairs at the hinge side. At full transparency the population cooperates at 0.84 instead of 0.92 (\cref{tab:hinge-body}), and below it the picture is less robust than on the floor side, for a reason that is visible in the matrix before any dynamics: on the hinge side \texttt{CupodBot}'s and \texttt{DupocBot}'s own-action rows coincide, so the behavioral family cannot tell them apart and spreads \texttt{DupocBot}'s mass over the two from the first step below $t = 1$. Under that family (\cref{table:tau-phase-body-hinge-side}, \cref{fig:tau-summary-body-hinge-side}) the stochastically stable set is \{\texttt{Cup}, \texttt{Dup}\} on all of $t \in [0.2, 0.8]$ for $\alpha \in \{0.4, 0.5\}$, with the population cooperating at 0.84 to 0.91 down to $t = 0.2$ as on the floor side (at $\alpha = 0.2$ the pair holds $t \in [0.4, 0.8]$ and \texttt{DefectBot} takes over at $t = 0.2$, as on the floor side); but at $\alpha = 0.6$ cooperation falls to 0.39 already at $t = 0.8$ (the static ceiling drops to 0.61 there) and to 0.04 at $t = 0.4$, and at $\alpha = 0.8$ it is not even monotone, 0.39 at $t = 0.8$ and 0.94 at $t = 0.6$ (a \texttt{TitForTatBot} monoculture) before collapsing at $t = 0.4$. The syntactic family does not confuse the two (their sources differ by one leaf) and gives the cleaner picture: cooperation survives down to $t = 0.8$ at $\alpha = 0.2$, $0.4$ at $\alpha = 0.4$, $0.2$ at $\alpha \in \{0.5, 0.6\}$ and $0.6$ at $\alpha = 0.8$, with the static and the dynamic collapse again coinciding at every $\alpha$. The two families disagree on the selected set at 19 of the 36 grid points, against 5 on the floor side.

So the robustness bracket is wider under transparency than at $t = 1$. What survives the hinge: cooperation is still what selection keeps at full transparency, still needs only a fraction of the information for a moderately cautious population ($\alpha = 0.5$: 20\% under either channel), and still collapses at the same transparency under the static and the dynamic criterion. What does not: the thresholds at both ends of the caution range. A very trusting population ($\alpha = 0.2$) needs 40\% on the floor side under either channel, and 80\% under source blur on the hinge side; a cautious one ($\alpha \ge 0.6$) needs 40 to 60\% on the floor side under either channel and, on the hinge side, 20 to 60\% under source blur but as much as full transparency under behavior blur. The reason is the row twin the hinge creates, not the dynamics, and a roster that separates \texttt{CupodBot} from \texttt{DupocBot} on the hinge side (the trolls rung does) would remove it.
