\paragraph{1. The body zoo has one twin pair.}
% REPLACES: "the body zoo is twin-free, so its behavioral transparency scale spans [0,1]".
The body zoo contains exactly one pair of twins. In PA-$S'$ the program \texttt{DIMCID} defects against every closed program, so at full transparency it is a payoff-exact twin of \texttt{DefectBot}: the two types have identical rows and identical columns in the payoff matrix, and identical stationary shares at all nine $(M,\beta)$ points of the Moran analysis. No other two of the ten types behave identically.
Two consequences follow. First, the behavioral channel cannot separate the pair at any blur level, so on this zoo its transparency scale has a ceiling strictly below one, while the syntactic channel, which reads the two different sources, still reaches full identification. Second, under the behavioral channel every stochastically stable set that contains \texttt{DefectBot} also contains \texttt{DIMCID}; the comparison of the two channels below must be read with this in mind, since the behavioral channel merges the two types by construction.

\paragraph{2. Where cooperation collapses.}
% REPLACES: "DupocBot is uniquely stochastically stable on t in [0.4,1] x alpha in [0.3,0.8]; the collapse sits between t = 0.4 and t = 0.2" and "the static and the dynamic collapse coincide at every alpha; stable faces vanish below the collapse".
On the body zoo under the behavioral channel, \texttt{DupocBot} is the unique stochastically stable type for every $t\in[0.2,1]$ at $\alpha\in\{0.3,0.45,0.62\}$, and for $t\in\{0.8,1\}$ at $\alpha=0.8$. For $\alpha\in\{0.45,0.62\}$ it loses this status only between $t=0.2$ and $t=0$; for $\alpha=0.3$ it keeps it down to $t=0$, with $66\%$ of the stationary mass. When caution is high ($\alpha=0.8$), \texttt{TitForTatBot} replaces it as the unique stochastically stable type at $t\in\{0.6,0.4\}$, followed at $t=0.2$ by a five-way tie of types that defect. With $\alpha=1$ no type is stochastically stable below $t=1$.
The identity of the selected type is not the level of cooperation, and it is the realized stationary cooperation rate that tracks the static analysis. For every $\alpha\geq 0.45$ and on both channels, realized cooperation collapses at exactly the grid point at which the fully cooperative Nash component disappears: wherever that component exists the realized rate is at least $0.87$, and wherever it is absent the realized rate is at most $0.04$, with one intermediate value ($0.22$ at $(t,\alpha)=(0.6,0.8)$ on the syntactic channel, where the best equilibrium still supports a cooperation rate of $0.61$). On the behavioral channel this collapse falls between $t=0.2$ and $t=0$ for $\alpha=0.45$, between $t=0.4$ and $t=0.2$ for $\alpha\in\{0.62,0.8\}$, and between $t=1$ and $t=0.8$ for $\alpha=1$.
The departure of the stochastically stable set from its full-transparency value is a different marker. On the syntactic channel it coincides with the static collapse at all five caution levels; on the behavioral channel it does so at two of four ($\alpha=0.45$ and $\alpha=1$). At $(t,\alpha)=(0.2,0.62)$ \texttt{DupocBot} is still the unique stochastically stable type, but as a defector: realized cooperation is $0.035$ and no cooperative equilibrium exists. At $\alpha=0.8$ the selected type changes to \texttt{TitForTatBot} at $t=0.6$ while cooperation persists ($0.950$ at $t=0.6$ and $0.874$ at $t=0.4$, with the fully cooperative component present).
One exception remains at low caution: at $\alpha=0.3$ on the behavioral channel the fully cooperative component survives at every $t$, while realized cooperation declines smoothly from $0.924$ at full transparency to $0.677$ at $t=0$.
Asymptotically stable face equilibria do not vanish with the collapse: on the behavioral channel four remain at $(0.2,0.62)$ and three at $(0.2,0.8)$ although no cooperative equilibrium exists there; none exists at $t=0$, nor at any $t<1$ for $\alpha=1$.

\paragraph{3. Zero transparency.}
% REPLACES: "at t = 0 the outcome is a tie among defecting types: {DefectBot, DupocBot, OBot} at alpha = 0.45 and {DefectBot, DupocBot, EBot, OBot} at alpha = 0.62".
At zero transparency the two channels yield the same payoff matrix for $\alpha\in\{0.45,0.62\}$. At $\alpha=0.45$ the stochastically stable set is the four-way tie $\{$\texttt{DIMCID}, \texttt{DefectBot}, \texttt{DupocBot}, \texttt{OBot}$\}$, each type holding $25.0\%$ of the stationary mass, and realized cooperation is zero. At $\alpha=0.62$ no type is stochastically stable: seven types (\texttt{CupodBot}, \texttt{OBot}, \texttt{TitForTatBot}, \texttt{DefectBot}, \texttt{DupocBot}, \texttt{EBot}, \texttt{DIMCID}) share the stationary mass equally, at $14.3\%$ each. At $\alpha=0.3$ the channels differ: \texttt{DupocBot} is selected with $66.0\%$ under the behavioral channel, \texttt{DefectBot} with $82.6\%$ under the syntactic one.
%
\paragraph{4. Blurring the source versus blurring behavior.}
% REPLACES: "at alpha = 0.8 cooperation dies at t* = 0.6 on the syntactic channel versus t* = 0.4 on the behavioral one".
At high caution ($\alpha=0.8$), blurring the source destroys cooperation two grid steps earlier than blurring behavior. At $t=0.6$ the realized stationary cooperation rate is $0.22$ under the syntactic channel and $0.95$ under the behavioral channel; at $t=0.4$ it is $0.01$ against $0.87$. The static analysis agrees: the fully cooperative Nash component is lost at $t=0.6$ under the syntactic channel (the best equilibrium supports a cooperation rate of $0.61$ there and of zero from $t=0.4$ on), and only at $t=0.2$ under the behavioral one.
The stochastically stable set does not show this gap: \texttt{DupocBot} loses unique stochastic stability at $t=0.6$ on both channels, to $\{$\texttt{DIMCID}, \texttt{DefectBot}$\}$ under the syntactic channel and to \texttt{TitForTatBot} under the behavioral one. Over the whole grid the two channels disagree, in the stochastically stable set or in the dominant replicator support, at $8$ of $36$ points on the body zoo and at $7$ of $36$ on the twelve-type zoo.

\paragraph{5. Updated numbers.}
% REPLACES (baseline): "0 pure ESS, 21 extreme NE in 3 components, 6 stable faces / 1013 supports, 3 invasion SCCs, {Coop, Dup, TFT} with a 95% basin, DupocBot's share rising 11% -> 89% (diagonal 27/56/82/88)"; the new diagonal is 24/55/80/87, see Table (a).
At full transparency the body zoo has no pure evolutionarily stable strategy, $33$ extreme Nash equilibria in $3$ connected components, $5$ asymptotically stable face equilibria among $1013$ supports, and an invasion graph with $4$ strongly connected components. The replicator dynamics reach the support $\{$\texttt{CooperateBot}, \texttt{DupocBot}, \texttt{TitForTatBot}$\}$ from $94\%$ of interior starting points. In the Moran process the stationary share of \texttt{DupocBot} rises with the strength of selection from $11\%$ at $(M,\beta)=(10,0.01)$ to $87\%$ at $(100,1)$ (Table~\ref{tab:sprime-moran-body}), and \texttt{DupocBot} is the unique stochastically stable type at six of the nine $(M,\beta)$ points. At $(100,1)$ the runner-up is \texttt{TitForTatBot} with $3.2\%$, followed by \texttt{DIMCID} and \texttt{DefectBot} with $3.0\%$ each. No pure evolutionarily stable strategy exists on any of the $95$ distinct payoff matrices of the six sweeps.
% REPLACES (aggregator ablation): "MaxConfidenceBot alone takes 89-90% in the mid-blur band".
In the mid-blur band of the aggregator ablation \texttt{MaxConfidenceBot} alone is stochastically stable, with $85\%$ of the stationary mass at $(t,\alpha)=(0.2,0.45)$ and $(0.2,0.62)$ and $87\%$ at $(0.6,0.8)$.
% REPLACES (zoo sensitivity): "29 of 30 twins-zoo points show a mixed {DupocBot, CIMCIC} dominant support".
Adding the two twins changes the stochastically stable set at $24$ of the $30$ grid points with $\alpha>0$, and $28$ of the $30$ points of the twelve-type zoo have a dominant replicator support containing both \texttt{DupocBot} and \texttt{CIMCIC}; at full transparency the two share the selected mass equally, with $45.6\%$ each.

\paragraph{6. Provenance of the cells.}
% REPLACES: "every matrix cell traces to a kernel-checked theorem (all 94 runs); the Nash cross-check is absent in 94/94 runs".
Every cell used in the experiments is a statement of PA-$S'$ checked by the Lean kernel, of one of two kinds. Most cells are outcome theorems. The others are \emph{floor dichotomies}: theorems without hypotheses that give one outcome if a named guard sentence has no proof within the budget and a different outcome if it has one. Which case holds is a question about the consistency of the theory for proofs of bounded length that is open in both directions, so neither is assumed: the experiments carry each floor at the outcome of its no-proof branch, and every run lists its floor cells.
The library contains $107$ outcome theorems and $26$ floor dichotomies. Among the twelve base programs of the three zoos, the $78$ unordered pairs consist of $65$ theorems and $13$ floors; on the body zoo the $55$ pairs consist of $51$ theorems and $4$ floors (\texttt{CupodBot} with \texttt{OBot}, and \texttt{DupocBot} with each of \texttt{CupodTrollBot}, \texttt{DBot} and \texttt{EBot}).
The six sweeps comprise $95$ distinct payoff matrices, each analysed once, and the integrity check passes on all $95$ runs. The secondary Nash solver used for cross-checking was not installed, so the cross-check is absent in all $95$ runs.
