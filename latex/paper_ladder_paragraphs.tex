\paragraph{A ladder of zoos.} \Cref{tab:zoo-ladder} runs the selection stages on four nested zoos. The seven-bot zoo \{\texttt{CooperateBot}, \texttt{DefectBot}, \texttt{TitForTatBot}, \texttt{DBot}, \texttt{EBot}, \texttt{OBot}, \texttt{DupocBot}\} is the six search-free bots plus the one Löbian cooperator, the smallest zoo in which the result of \cref{subsubsection:Results-Base-Analysis} can be stated; \texttt{body} adds \texttt{CupodBot}; the ten-bot zoo adds the two name-reading bots, \texttt{CupodTrollBot} and \texttt{DupocTrollBot}, which defect exactly against the literal \texttt{CupodBot} and the literal \texttt{DupocBot} respectively and cooperate with everyone else, one troll per Löbian archetype; the twelve-bot zoo adds the two implication provers \texttt{CIMCIC} and \texttt{DIMDID}, discussed below. The selection answer is the same on the first three: \texttt{DupocBot} is the stochastically stable strategy at strong selection, with 86\% to 88\% of the long run, selected at eight of the nine $(M,\beta)$ points, and no troll ever holds more than 1\% at $(100, 1)$. Adding \texttt{CupodBot} to the seven leaves \texttt{DupocBot}'s share unchanged; adding the trolls raises it by two points, since \texttt{CupodTrollBot} is one more partner it exploits. What the trolls do change is the geometry below the long run: both are exploited by \texttt{DefectBot}, \texttt{DBot} and \texttt{EBot}, so they feed the defectors near the all-defect face, and the replicator basin of \{\texttt{DefectBot}, \texttt{OBot}\}, empty on the smaller zoos, takes 27\% of the starts while the cooperative basin falls from 88\% to 65\%. Where a population starts now matters; where it ends in the long run does not. The result is not a property of the eight we happened to pick.

\paragraph{The implication provers, and the troll's twin.} The last rung adds the two coherent implication guards: \texttt{CIMCIC} (cooperate if I can prove that my cooperation implies yours) and \texttt{DIMDID} (defect if I can prove that my defection implies yours), each other's C/D transposition, and the polarity that, unlike \texttt{DIMCID}'s, does not collapse (item 3 of \cref{subsubsection:Results-FullTrans-Outcomes}). \texttt{CIMCIC}'s row of actions equals \texttt{DupocBot}'s on the engine side, so it is the sensitivity test of the thesis, a behavioral twin added to the zoo; but its column does not, because \texttt{DupocTrollBot} defects against the literal \texttt{DupocBot} and cooperates with \texttt{CIMCIC}. Selection notices: the stochastically stable set becomes \{\texttt{CIMCIC}, \texttt{DupocBot}\} at eight of the nine points, with 93.1\% of the long run at strong selection, and the split is no longer exact, 48.5\% to 44.6\% in \texttt{CIMCIC}'s favor. A troll that targets a program by name hands the advantage to any program that behaves the same under another name; the all-defect basin the trolls had opened closes again (98\% of the starts now reach the cooperative support), and the long run spends 97\% of its time in self-cooperating states. \texttt{DIMDID} is the other story. Its guard is coherent, so it is a genuine strategy, one that cooperates by default and defects only when it can prove that defection would be reciprocated; against this zoo that makes it cooperate with the simulators and the trolls, defect on \texttt{DefectBot}, \texttt{OBot} and \texttt{CupodBot}, and get exploited by both Löbian cooperators, whose guards it cannot satisfy and whose defection it cannot prove (two hinge cells, $(C,D)$ on the engine side). It holds 7.7\% at the weakest point and nothing at strong selection. On the hinge side those two cells become $(D,D)$, \texttt{DIMDID} joins the unexploitable set, and its share is still zero: not being exploited is not enough; a strategy must also be cooperated with. The only strategy that displaces \texttt{DupocBot} on any rung is its own behavioral twin.
